About this signal

What this model is trying to do, and what it isn't.

Purpose

The goal here is not to maximize returns. It's to sidestep the worst outcomes. The model tries to identify when SPX has entered a persistently unfavorable (bearish) regime, so that instead of riding out a severe drawdown, you can hold cash or reallocate elsewhere until conditions improve. Historically this has meant giving up some upside during choppy or ambiguous periods, in exchange for avoiding the deepest, most damaging drawdowns (2008, 2020, and similar episodes).

How to read the signal

The label shown on the signal page is a forecast for the next trading session, built from data through the most recent close — it is not a statement about the day already shown. If the page says "Bearish" as of a given date, that reflects the model's read on the session that follows, based on everything known up to that date's close.

Limitations

  • No transaction costs or slippage. The backtest assumes frictionless 0/1 switching. Actual turnover from a six-month retrain cadence plus regime flips is not free, and can meaningfully erode the numbers shown.
  • No liquidity modeling. Position changes are assumed to execute at the closing price with unlimited size.
  • Single asset. This is built and tested on SPX alone. It has not been validated as a general-purpose regime signal for other assets.
  • Model instability near the live edge. The most recent walk-forward windows can be noisier than the long-run historical average — treat recent regime flips with appropriate skepticism.
